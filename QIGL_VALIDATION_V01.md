Q-IGL simulations do not support a universal advantage of global, hierarchical, or task-selected structured controllers. They do support three narrower design conclusions: correlated structure can be exploitable; stale/wrong structural models can be harmful; and control effort must be evaluated relative to task-sensitive directions. In the final preregistered H4 test, a simple full-vector adaptive baseline outperformed all structured controllers. Therefore persistent memory should store contextual performance, uncertainty and provenance rather than enforce a fixed structural ontology.
